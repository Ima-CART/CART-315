Reflective reading 1: The Stuff Games Are Made Of (P. Barr) - Chapter 2

Pippin Barr’s The stuff games are made of, chapter 2: Rules: Changes come from within reminds me of the game design class I took in the summer. Barr talked about changing the rules of a game and its outcome. He took a simple game Pong and added 36 variations just by asking questions. Wondering what would happen to the game of Pong if the paddle was no longer visible. Reduce the opacity of the paddle and the balls passes through. Looking at the change, I wondered what kind of game it would be, if no one hits the ball and the game continues.  Another example the Vienna Pong, just asking one question; Are you in Vienna? Yes or No. Answering no and that is the end but answer yes and a normal game of Pong starts.  Question gave rise to different experimentation and user experience.
My game design course tasked me with the traditional game snakes and ladders. I was tasked to take simple traditional game and change it. My snakes and ladders was a team based, turn order game with hidden and exposed victory points with a ladder climbing mechanic. To give birth to my idea, I needed to think how the game would be if people worked together. How would the game change if there was a turn order? What if there was more to the game then just racing to the top? What if you can collect points? What if landing on the ladder gave you a bonus reward? Or if sliding down a snake causes you to have a penalty? All these questions gave rise to my snake and ladder variation. It did not stop there; my teacher wanted his students to update the variation they made. I needed to figure out what worked and what didn’t and then update it. The process of game design doesn’t stop. I could have gone deeper as more questions and idea can continue to change a game. I understood the feeling of talking to a game and developing it to make it different for myself and those who play my game. Game design doesn’t stop at any rules, it continues to evolve.
